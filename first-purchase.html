My First Purchase: What I Wish I'd Known

July 3, 2026 6 min read Guides

Two years ago, I spent weeks going back and forth before finally deciding. It wasn't really about the price, or even what anyone else might think — it was that I couldn't find an honest review anywhere, written by someone who wasn't trying to sell me something. So I ended up learning as I went, with a few missteps along the way. Here's what I wish I'd read before ordering.

TPE or Silicone: The First Question Everyone Asks

Both materials have their fans, and honestly, there's no wrong answer here — just what fits your budget and your priorities best.

TPE (thermoplastic elastomer) feels softer to the touch, costs less, and is often the natural starting point for a first purchase. The trade-off is that it's more porous: it needs more regular care and it's a bit less durable over time.

Silicone generally costs more, but it's non-porous, easier to clean, and holds fine detail (texture, finishing) much better over the long run. It's an investment, but a lot of people in the community feel it pays off if the budget allows.

My advice: if this is your first purchase and you're not sure how you'll feel about it long-term, TPE remains a solid way to try things out without committing too much.

Size and Weight — the Part Everyone Underestimates

This is probably where I miscalculated the most. You look at photos, you imagine, and you completely forget two very practical things: where am I going to store this and can I actually handle it on my own.

Larger models (over 30 kg / roughly 65 lbs) require real planning: a discreet storage space, sometimes a dedicated cover or case, and enough physical strength to move it safely. If you live with roommates or discretion matters a lot to you, more compact formats or torsos are often much easier to fit into daily life.

Budget: What Actually Changes Between Price Ranges

Without giving exact figures (they shift depending on the brand and the season), here's what I've noticed across the tiers:

  • Entry-level: fine for getting started, but the finish and long-term durability are more limited.
  • Mid-range: the best value for most people, with a more reliable material and some customization options.
  • High-end: much finer detail (texture, articulation, realism), but the price jump is only worth it if those details genuinely matter to you.

My mistake was trying to save money on entry-level for what I thought would be occasional use — and ultimately wishing I'd spent a bit more on something built to last.

Discreet Shipping: The Real Question Nobody Wants to Ask

This is usually the biggest hesitation before ordering. Good news: most reputable sellers in this space use plain, unmarked packaging with no indication of contents, and discreet billing on your bank statement. Before ordering, it's always worth checking this directly on the site or with customer service — a seller who answers this clearly is usually a good sign.

Care, the Simple Version

Nothing complicated, but it does take consistency:

  • Clean after every use with a product made for the material (not just any soap)
  • Dry completely before storing, to avoid moisture buildup
  • Store flat or hanging, depending on the model, away from direct light

Skipping this routine is the fastest way to shorten the product's lifespan — something I learned the hard way in year one.

What I'd Do Differently

If I were starting over, I'd take more time comparing materials instead of choosing on price alone, and I'd think through storage before picking a size. Nothing dramatic, but it would have saved me some frustration those first few months.
